[Gutenberg](https://github.com/OxfordRSE/gutenberg) is the open-source platform the Oxford Research Software Engineering group (OxRSE) uses to publish training material and run training events. You can see it in action at [train.rse.ox.ac.uk](https://train.rse.ox.ac.uk).

All of our training material has always been freely available to read on Gutenberg. Until now, though, the only way to work through it as a structured, tracked programme was to attend one of our events. Version 2.0 changes that with **Courses**: curated learning pathways through the material that anyone with an account can follow at their own pace, with the same progress tracking that our instructor-led events have always had.

![The courses catalogue]({{ "/assets/images/whats-new/courses.png" | relative_url }})

## Courses: learn at your own pace

Previously you could browse and read any of the material, but on your own it was just a library of pages: there was no suggested route through it and no record of what you had done. Learning pathways and progress tracking only existed inside scheduled events. Courses bring that structure to self-paced learning, so you don't need to wait for the next event to get it.

- **Browse** a catalogue of courses, with search and filters for level, tag and language.
- **Enrol** in the courses you want.
- **Work through** the material in order, at whatever pace suits you.
- **Track your progress** as you go and see when you have completed a course.

At launch there are 26 courses based on [the HPC-universe material](https://github.com/HPC-universe/course-material), covering Python, C++, Git, testing, HPC, MPI and OpenMP, numerical methods, cloud computing and more. [Browse the courses](https://train.rse.ox.ac.uk/courses), or read more in the [courses guide]({{ "/guide/courses/" | relative_url }}).

## A new home page

![The home page showing enrolled courses and progress]({{ "/assets/images/whats-new/home.png" | relative_url }})

The home page has been redesigned around what you are currently learning. It shows your enrolled courses and events with your progress in each, so you can pick up where you left off.

A new switcher in the sidebar lets you move between your active course and your active event. Whichever one you choose shapes the rest of the site. See [learning context]({{ "/guide/learning-context/" | relative_url }}) for details.

## Smarter navigation through material

Material pages now understand which course or event you are working through:

- previous and next links follow the course or event structure, and the dependencies between material
- hints on each page show where it fits in your current course
- when you reach the end of a course, you are taken back to the course page

See [material navigation]({{ "/guide/material-navigation/" | relative_url }}) for more.

## Events

Events are still how we run scheduled, instructor-led training, and they now sit alongside courses. An instructor can create a new event from scratch or use an existing course as a blueprint, so a cohort can be run through a self-paced course's material. See the [events guide]({{ "/guide/events/" | relative_url }}) and [how to create an event from a course]({{ "/how-to/create-event-from-course/" | relative_url }}).

## For instructors and admins

Creating and maintaining courses and events is simpler too (see [how to create a self-paced course]({{ "/how-to/create-self-paced-course/" | relative_url }})):

- **Editing on a single page.** Course and event material is arranged in groups, and you can add, remove and reorder it inline without moving between pages.
- **Progress statistics.** Admins can see enrolment, in-progress and completion counts for every course and event, with a detail page for each.
- **Default course sync.** Admins can load the default course catalogue from the courses page. When the defaults change, Gutenberg shows a clear diff, broken down by material group, so you can review the changes before applying them. See [how to sync default courses]({{ "/how-to/sync-default-courses/" | relative_url }}).
- **JSON import and export.** Courses can be exported and imported as JSON. This makes it easy to move them between Gutenberg instances or edit them outside the application.
